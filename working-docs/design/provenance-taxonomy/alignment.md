# Alignment: PROV-O, SOSA, SSN and SIO

See also: [README.md](README.md) (the claim model),
[asserter.md](asserter.md), [basis.md](basis.md),
[method.md](method.md), [source.md](source.md), [change.md](change.md),
[conflict.md](conflict.md), [examples/](examples/README.md),
[mappings/](mappings/pitloom-provenance.sssom.tsv) (some of the same terms
as SSSOM rows),
[vocabularies-considered.md](vocabularies-considered.md) (these five and
the others weighed, one entry each).

**Status:** draft; reviewed once and corrected, 2026-11-16. The four
vocabularies explain the model and are mapping targets. None is written
into an SBOM: the provenance strings and the Annotation records keep their
own keys. ODRL, a fifth, is read for licence claims only and has its own
section.

## Texts read

Read 2026-11-16, from the published ontology files and specifications.

| Vocabulary | Text | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PROV-O | `http://www.w3.org/ns/prov-o`, version 2013-04-30; PROV-Constraints for one inference | W3C Recommendations |
| SOSA, SSN | `https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-ssn/`, 2017-10-19 | W3C Recommendation, with OGC |
| SOSA to PROV-O | `https://www.w3.org/ns/sosa/prov/`; section 6.5 of the above | not normative |
| SIO | `http://semanticscience.org/ontology/sio.owl`, version 1.62 | a community ontology |
| ODRL | `https://www.w3.org/ns/odrl/2/ODRL22.ttl`, version 2.2; the Information Model, 2018-02-15 | W3C Recommendation; its common vocabulary is not normative |

## What each one answers

| Vocabulary | Its question | What it lacks for this model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PROV-O | who is responsible for a thing, what made it, what it came from | no word for the basis of a value; nothing for a value that was not obtained |
| SOSA | how a value was observed: by what, of what, by which procedure | no class for a value that is stated and not observed; one class for every observation |
| SSN | SOSA with more: systems, stimuli, capabilities | the same |
| SIO | what kind of proposition a value is, and what supports or disputes it | no relation for who asserted a proposition |

SOSA can be used with PROV-O: the SSN specification gives an alignment,
not normative, in which `sosa:Observation` is a subclass of
`prov:Activity`, `sosa:Sensor` of `prov:Agent` and `prov:Entity`,
`sosa:Procedure` of `prov:Plan`, `sosa:Result`, `sosa:FeatureOfInterest`
and `sosa:ObservableProperty` of `prov:Entity`, and `sosa:isResultOf` is a
sub-property of `prov:wasGeneratedBy`, `sosa:madeBySensor` of
`prov:wasAssociatedWith`, and `sosa:hasFeatureOfInterest` of `prov:used`.
An observation is an activity with more detail. The alignment covers SOSA
terms only, not `ssn:Stimulus` or `ssn:isProxyFor`.

SIO is a separate upper ontology. Its file links two terms to PROV-O by
annotation (`is alternate of` to `prov:alternateOf`, `is specialization of`
to `prov:specializationOf`) and holds no other mapping to PROV-O, SOSA or
SSN. No reasoner was run over the four together. SIO terms are used here
as loose mapping rows only.

## The pattern

Two entities and two acts. The model records the facets, not these nodes.

```text
recorded value (prov:Entity): what Pitloom wrote
  |
  |-- prov:wasGeneratedBy --> Pitloom's act (prov:Activity; no key)
  |      |-- prov:used ---------------> the source          Source
  |      |-- prov:wasAssociatedWith --> Pitloom, its tool   Tool
  |      `-- the plan of that act ----> class, technique    Method, Technique
  |
  `-- prov:wasDerivedFrom --> statement (prov:Entity): the value as its
        |                     asserter gave it, inside the source
        |   prov:wasQuotedFrom   when Pitloom copied it unchanged
        |   prov:wasRevisionOf   when Pitloom changed it     Written, Change
        |
        |-- prov:atLocation -------> where in the source     Location
        |-- prov:wasAttributedTo --> party                   Asserter
        `-- prov:wasGeneratedBy ---> the asserter's own act  Basis
```

Why two entities. PROV-Constraints says an entity "can only be ascribed to
an agent if the agent was associated with an activity that generated the
entity" (inference 13). A value attributed to the originator cannot then
be an entity generated by Pitloom's reading. The originator's statement is
one entity; what Pitloom wrote down, derived from it, is another.

**Asserter** is the attribution of the statement.

**Basis** says what is known of the asserter's own act:

- not known: `declared`. PROV-O says of attribution that the entity "was
  generated by some unspecified activity" and that the relation "is useful
  when the activity is not known, or irrelevant";
- known, and it examined the thing itself: `measured` when the act is
  Pitloom's and exact, `detected` otherwise. Such an act is a
  `sosa:Observation`;
- known, and it concluded the value from something else, or by judgement:
  `inferred`.

The lines between `measured`, `detected` and `inferred` are this model's
own. SOSA has one class for every observation. `ssn:isProxyFor` does not
mark `inferred`: in SSN every stimulus is a proxy for the property, and
its example is a thermometer ("the expansion of quicksilver is a stimulus
that serves as a proxy for some temperature property").

**Method**, **Technique** and **Tool** describe Pitloom's act. The plan is
reached through `prov:hadPlan` on the association of act and agent, or
`sosa:usedProcedure` when the act is an observation.

When the asserter is `sbom-tool` the statement and the recorded value are
one entity and the two acts are one. That is why `sbom-tool` takes its
default basis from the method class ([basis.md](basis.md), rule
5).

A relay is one hop further: the statement Pitloom read in a registry is
itself `prov:wasQuotedFrom` the originator's own, which Pitloom did not
read. The model keeps that hop in the source kind, not as a record.

## Facet by facet

| This model | PROV-O | SOSA, SSN | SIO (loose) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| recorded value | `prov:Entity`, with `prov:value` | `sosa:Result` when Pitloom observed it | `proposition` |
| the statement behind it | `prov:Entity`, by `prov:wasDerivedFrom` | - | `statement` |
| element | `prov:Entity` | `sosa:FeatureOfInterest` | - |
| field | `prov:Entity` (by the alignment) | `sosa:ObservableProperty` | - |
| `Asserter` | `prov:wasAttributedTo` a `prov:Agent` | - | nearest: `has creator`, `has provider` |
| `sbom-tool`, `ai-agent` | `prov:SoftwareAgent` | - | - |
| `Method` class with `Technique` | `prov:Plan` | `sosa:Procedure` | `plan`, under `action specification` |
| Pitloom's act (no key) | `prov:Activity` | `sosa:Observation` when it examined the element | `measuring`, `observing` |
| `Tool` | `prov:SoftwareAgent`, by `prov:wasAssociatedWith` | `sosa:Sensor`; Pitloom is the `sosa:Platform` | - |
| `Basis: declared` | the asserter's act is not known | "asserted"; no class | `statement` |
| `Basis: measured`, `detected` | the asserter's act is known | `sosa:Observation` | `measurement value`, for a quantity |
| `Basis: inferred` | the asserter's act is known | - | `conclusion` |
| `Basis: lower-bound` | - | nearest: a value beyond `ssn-system:MeasurementRange` | - |
| `Basis: not-measured` | - | - | - |
| `Source` | `prov:used` by Pitloom's act | - | `has source` |
| `Location` | `prov:atLocation` | - | - |
| a value copied unchanged; a relay | `prov:wasQuotedFrom` | - | `quote` |
| `Written`, `Change` | `prov:wasRevisionOf` | - | `is subsequent version of` |
| two candidates for one field | `prov:alternateOf` (no grade) | - | `is alternate of` |
| agreement; the grade of a conflict | - | - | `is supported by`; `is disputed by`, `is refuted by` |
| a concluded licence with its `Note` | - | - | `conclusion`, `justification` |

Every row is a related, close or broad match, never exact. The SSSOM file
holds a row, with its predicate, for most of these terms and not for all.

SIO's `conclusion` appears twice, for two things that are not one concept:
a value an asserter inferred, and a licence Pitloom concluded.

## Acting on behalf of

`prov:actedOnBehalfOf` relates a subordinate agent to the agent
responsible: "the subordinate agent acted on behalf of the responsible
agent in an actual activity". It is the relation behind
[asserter.md](asserter.md) rule 3 (a tool's output belongs to whoever ran
it).

It names a question for the rows below; it does not decide them. It finds
the party responsible for an act. Which asserter that party is depends on
its relation to the element, and on whether the act made the statement or
only read one. The model does not fold `sbom-tool` or `ai-agent` into
`sbom-author`, though Pitloom acts for the SBOM author.

| Who wrote the value | On whose behalf | Asserter | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a build backend writing `PKG-INFO` | the project | `originator` | in the draft |
| a lock tool writing a pin | the project | `originator` | in the draft |
| a lock tool writing the hash of a dependency's file | the project | `other-party` | in the draft: the project is not the maker of that file |
| LicenseID, run by Pitloom, matching the element's licence file | Pitloom | `originator` | ruled (rule 5): the match reads a statement, it makes none |
| a training tool writing a model card (OX-4 in the examples) | the model's maker | `originator` | in the draft; basis `declared` (ruled 2026-11-23) |
| `pipdeptree`, started by Pitloom: which versions are installed, the dependency tree | Pitloom | `sbom-tool` | ruled 2026-11-23, with basis `measured`; the field table said `other-party` until then |
| a registry recording a yank, a gate or an owner's namespace | its own; the act it records is the maintainer's | `other-party` | ruled 2026-11-23, with basis `declared`: the registry's record |

For the two rows that were open (both ruled 2026-11-23):

- **P1. `pipdeptree`: `sbom-tool`, `measured`** (ruled). Rule 3 gives the
  asserter: Pitloom started the tool, so its output is Pitloom's. No
  other party states which versions are installed; the tool asks the
  environment itself, and any correct tool gets the same list and the
  same tree from it, which is the test of `measured`
  ([basis.md](basis.md)). The Core Metadata fields it hands on stay
  `originator` (the field table's other row). The row of the field
  table changed from `other-party`, `detected`. A weak spot, kept
  in view: the tree rests on one rule for reading `Requires-Dist`
  against the installed set.
- **P2. A registry's record of a maintainer's act stays `other-party`,
  `declared`** (ruled). A yank, a gate, an owner's namespace: the API field
  does not say who acted, so the one party Pitloom knows to have said
  so is the registry. A commit that a ref names is `originator`
  (ruled 2026-11-23) for another reason: a ref is content of the
  repository, and these are fields of the registry's own database.
  This is what the field table and the examples (PD-1, PD-3, PD-5)
  said already. Not taken: `originator`, as the maintainer's act
  relayed. A weak spot, kept in view: the reason of a yank is as a
  rule the maintainer's own words, and is recorded as the registry's.

## Beside the model: ODRL, for licence claims

ODRL describes what a policy permits: a `Permission`, `Prohibition` or
`Duty` over an `Asset`, between parties. That is the content of a licence,
not where a claim came from, so ODRL is no part of the pattern above.
Three of its parts lie near decisions of this model for the licence field.

| ODRL | Its text | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `odrl:Offer`, with `odrl:assigner` | "a Policy that proposes a Rule over an Asset from an assigner"; the assigner is "the issuer of the Rule" | the originator's own licence statement: `originator`, the declared licence |
| `odrl:Assertion` (common vocabulary, not normative) | "asserts a Rule over an Asset from parties"; it "does not grant such permissions/prohibitions but only asserts the parties claims" | a licence claim that proposes nothing: a conclusion by `sbom-tool` or `sbom-author`; loosely, a claim by `other-party` or `ai-agent` |
| `odrl:conflict`, with `perm`, `prohibit`, `invalid` | "the conflict-resolution strategy for a Policy"; `invalid` voids the policy and is the default | named rules, as in [conflict.md](conflict.md); `invalid` is the nearest to `no-rule` |

Limits of these three:

- An Offer grants nothing either ("does not grant any Rules"); the policy
  that grants is `odrl:Agreement`. The line between Offer and Assertion is
  proposing against claiming.
- The note on `odrl:Assertion` speaks of "a party (an assignee or
  assigner)" that claims "what terms they have over an Asset". A registry
  or an agent is neither, so those two rows are a stretch. A licensee
  doing so is close to BSI's effective licence (under which the SBOM's
  creator uses the component), the stage facet
  [conflict.md](conflict.md) reserves.
- The three strategies are the core values, not a closed list:
  "Additional conflict property values MAY be defined by ODRL Profiles".

`odrl:partOf` (an asset as a member of a collection) could describe the
scope of a licence file (the project, a folder), deferred
(ruled 2026-11-23) in
[asserter-licence.md](asserter-licence.md).

What ODRL does not cover:

- Its conflict is a permission against a prohibition on one asset, for
  the same action or one that includes the other; its example sets a
  `use` permission of one policy against a `print` prohibition of another,
  from two assigners. A disagreement over which licence applies to a
  thing is outside it.
- `-only` against `-or-later` is not an ODRL conflict as written: "a later
  version" is not an action. It would be one only if each licence were
  written as a policy, which Pitloom does not do.
- The ODRL and SPDX files read do not mention each other. ODRL's common
  vocabulary embeds Creative Commons terms. Whether other parties publish
  ODRL renderings of the SPDX licences was not searched.

## Limits

- PROV-O has no word for the basis of a value and names no kinds of
  revision. It
  tells a known act from an unknown one, no more.
- Nothing in the four says a value was not obtained and why
  (`not-measured` with its reason). These stay Pitloom's own.
- SOSA and SSN have no stated value; `declared` is described by PROV-O,
  with SIO's `statement` as a loose match.
- SIO's three evidence relations run from a proposition to anything that
  bears on it, not from claim to claim. They have no declared domain or
  range and are not declared symmetric. Their definition texts are loose
  (the text of `is refuted by` opens with the name of its parent).
- A full PROV-O record per field value is too heavy to write. The pattern
  is a model to check the facets against, not a serialisation.
